Good morning, and a very pleasant Sunday to you. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. The National Democratic Congress has ordered all aspirants participating in its ongoing reorganisation to take down oversized campaign posters and banners within five days. According to reports from Starr F M and The Ghana Report, the party's Functional Executive Committee has set strict size limits of five feet wide by eight feet high, warning aspirants to comply starting from October tenth.

Story two. Amid discussions about joining the B R I C S bloc, international relations analyst Dr. Elvis Botah has cautioned that membership will not resolve Ghana's economic difficulties without a deliberate industrial strategy. Reporting by Three News indicates that Dr. Botah warned the country could face economic exploitation, benefiting far less than major powers such as China, India, Brazil, and Russia.

Story three. Ghana's Minority in Parliament has demanded the immediate sacking of the Minister for Sports and Recreation, Kofi Adams, alongside the Chief Executive Officer of the Ghana Tourism Authority, over a recent visa scandal. Three News reports that policy analyst Kofi Bentil has also weighed in on the controversy, insisting that more high-ranking officials must be held accountable.

Story four. President John Mahama has visited the Ahafo Region to inspect the Bechem Meat Processing Factory. According to Three News, the facility is equipped to process six thousand birds per hour across two production lines to boost local poultry supply.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In Saudi Arabia, an attack on the international airport in the capital, Riyadh, has killed twelve people and injured over three hundred others. The Guardian, B B C News, and Al Jazeera report that authorities have blamed Yemen's Houthi rebels for the strike, prompting United States President Donald Trump to state that his country is considering joining Saudi retaliatory actions.

Story six. A drone attack by a Sudanese paramilitary group has killed forty-one people near the border with Ethiopia, including eight children. According to B B C News Africa, a rights group reported that the deadly strike directly hit a gathering of displaced civilians.
