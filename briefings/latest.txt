Good afternoon, and welcome to your political news briefing for this Monday. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. Graphic Online, MyJoyOnline, and The Ghana Report note that following the National Democratic Congress constituency executive elections across two hundred seventy-six constituencies, party National Chairman Johnson Asiedu Nketiah has urged unsuccessful candidates to accept the results and close ranks. Mr Asiedu Nketiah commended members for the peaceful conduct of the polls, which serve as the foundation for the N D C re-election architecture for two thousand twenty-eight.

Story two. According to MyJoyOnline, the Chairman of the New Patriotic Party’s Disciplinary Committee, Joseph Osei-Owusu, also known as Joe Wise, has stated that the party is willing to welcome former Trade Minister Alan Kyerematen back as a member. However, Joe Wise made it clear that the N P P will not consider Mr Kyerematen for the flagbearer position at this stage.

Story three. Graphic Online reports that All People’s Congress founder Hassan Ayariga has called on the government to subject Ghana’s proposed participation in B R I C S to parliamentary scrutiny and broad public consultation. He warned that any binding international commitments without Parliament's involvement raise fundamental questions about constitutional governance and national sovereignty.

Story four. Starr FM reports that an investigator testifying in court has described Regional Chairman Wontumi as the critical culprit in the Tano Nimiri illegal mining investigations. The investigator alleged that Wontumi aided illegal mining activities within the forest reserve.

Now, around the world.

Story five. BBC News and Africanews report that Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva has clinched re-election to a fourth non-consecutive term after a dramatic runoff victory over Flávio Bolsonaro. Despite the results, Bolsonaro has called for the vote to be annulled and refused to concede.

Story six. France 24 and Africanews report that Israelis are preparing to head to the polls in the country’s first national elections since the October seventh attacks. The deeply divisive vote is widely viewed as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s leadership and national security policies.
