Good morning. It is Saturday, October seventeen.

First, the news from home.

Story one. Heavy criticism continues to trail the appointment of National Democratic Congress activist Emelia Affi Agbenyo as Deputy Chairperson of the Electoral Commission. Three News reports that election observer group C O D E O, governance expert Professor Baffour Agyeman Duah, and private legal practitioner Martin Kpebu have strongly condemned the move, with Kpebu warning that the decision undermines the independence of the commission and urging her to resign.

Story two. The New Patriotic Party is rallying its base for future elections, with leading figures making strong calls for internal party cohesion. According to MyJoyOnline and The Ghana Report, the party held a National Islamic Thanksgiving Service in Kintampo where Flagbearer Dr Mahamudu Bawumia urged unity ahead of twenty twenty-eight, while Member of Parliament Sammi Awuku delivered a similar appeal against division to party faithful in Germany.

Story three. A spokesperson for Kennedy Agyapong has dismissed claims that the politician has left the governing New Patriotic Party. Starr F M reports that the spokesperson issued a firm clarification confirming that Agyapong has not resigned from the N P P, putting an end to recent unverified rumors.

Story four. Former Member of Parliament Ras Mubarak has criticized the Israeli Ambassador to Ghana over remarks made about President John Mahama. Starr F M reports that Mubarak described the envoy's comments as disrespectful and called on the Minister for Foreign Affairs, Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa, to formally summon the ambassador over the matter.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In North Africa, Morocco has made political history with the swearing-in of its new administration. Africanews reports that King Mohammed the Sixth has formally appointed the country's government, which is led for the very first time by a woman Prime Minister, Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri.

Story six. In the Democratic Republic of Congo, political tensions have spiked after the government banned the activities of thirteen political parties, including the P P R D led by former President Joseph Kabila. Africanews reports that while authorities justify the move as a national security measure, opposition figures have fiercely criticized the ban as political repression.
