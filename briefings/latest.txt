Good afternoon, and happy Friday. 

First, the news from home. 

Story one, according to Starr FM, the Economic and Organized Crime Office has granted the Member of Parliament for Manhyia South, Nana Agyei Baffour Awuah, a fifty million Ghana cedis bail after his arrest over allegations including money laundering and financial loss. Majority Leader Afenyo-Markin has urged state agencies and the office to focus on drug barons and galamsey networks instead of opposition voices, demanding his immediate and unconditional release. 

Story two, MyJoyOnline reports that the New Patriotic Party will establish thirty-one voting centres at the Baba Yara Sports Stadium for its upcoming national executive elections to speed up the voting process. According to the party's Director of Elections, Evans Nimako, the arrangement aims to prevent voting from extending into the night for the more than seven thousand delegates expected to participate. 

Story three, Graphic Online reports that the Member of Parliament for Builsa South, Doctor Clement Abas Apaak, has commended all aspirants, delegates, and officials for the successful completion of the vetting process for the upcoming constituency elections of the National Democratic Congress. He congratulated those who successfully went through and urged a peaceful and productive campaign focused on ideas, programmes, and records. 

Story four, Three News reports that Bank of Ghana Governor Asiama stated that the current regime, where over sixty percent of total banking assets accrue to foreign-owned banks, may not be very optimal from a strategic point of view. He also noted that private sector credit growth rebounded to thirty-five point five percent in August this year. 

Now, around the world. 

Story five, according to France Twenty-Four, Ethiopia and Eritrea have cut all diplomatic ties after Ethiopia ordered the closure of its embassy in Eritrea and declared ten Eritrean diplomats persona non grata. The diplomatic fallout follows overnight blasts in Addis Ababa and escalating conflict involving rebel fighters in northern Ethiopia. 

Story six, Al Jazeera reports that support for German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has plunged to a record low approval rating after his Christian Democratic Union party suffered defeats in three state elections.
