Good evening. Today is Thursday, October eight, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. The New Patriotic Party is mourning the death of its former National Chairman, Samuel Arthur Odoi-Sykes, who has passed away at the age of eighty-seven. Graphic Online reports that the diplomat, lawyer, and former Member of Parliament has received warm tributes from party leaders, including the N P P flag bearer, Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia.

Story two. Following the N P P's national executive elections, party leaders are calling for immediate reconciliation to build a united front. Graphic Online reports that Ashanti Regional Chairman Odeneho Kwaku Appiah stressed the importance of unity, while The Ghana Report notes that Member of Parliament Charles Forson dismissed concerns that past rivalries between the new chairman and general secretary would hinder their collaboration.

Story three. The Ranking Member of Parliament's Foreign Affairs Committee, Samuel Abu Jinapor, has urged the government to carefully examine Ghana's bid to join the B R I C S alliance. MyJoyOnline reports that Jinapor cautioned against expecting a free lunch, warning that the potential benefits of joining the bloc could come with risks to the country's national interests.

Story four. Parliament's Public Accounts Committee has summoned a senior medical officer to explain an unearned salary payment of over three hundred and forty-one thousand Ghana cedis. According to 3News, Doctor Mockshell Esinam Sunu was paid the money despite failing to proceed on her approved study leave.

Now, around the world.

Story five. Tensions are rising in East Africa after Ethiopian government forces launched drone attacks against Eritrean troops who crossed into the Tigray region. The Guardian reports that the military incursions have heightened fears of a renewed regional war between the neighboring nations.

Story six. In West Africa, Guinea-Bissau's transition back to civilian rule has hit a major roadblock. Africanews reports that the national electoral commission announced that conditions have not been met to hold the legislative and presidential elections planned for December.
