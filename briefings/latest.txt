Good afternoon, and happy Saturday. 

First, the news from home. 

Story one. According to MyJoyOnline and Starr FM, Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin has accused the governing the N D C of adopting double standards on accountability. He insisted that the party must accept the exact same scrutiny it demanded of the previous N P P administration.

Story two, reported by Graphic Online, is that the youth wing of the opposition the N P P has called on President John Dramani Mahama to dismiss Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa. The party alleged financial irregularities in the evacuation of Ghanaians from South Africa and the sale of diplomatic passports, demanding an independent investigation.

Story three, from Graphic Online, reveals that the Gender Centre for Empowering Development has warned that vote-buying is threatening new entrants in party contests. The group stated that money politics is locking out first-time aspirants, young people, and women from internal party elections.

Now, around the world. 

Story four. According to the Guardian and France Twenty-Four, the traditional family photo at the G-twenty foreign ministers meeting was abruptly cancelled. This happened after about one-third of the participating countries, including the United Kingdom and Australia, refused to appear beside Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov.

Story five, reported by Al Jazeera and France Twenty-Four, details that the United Nations has officially closed its mission in Somalia, ending three decades of political engagement. Somalia hailed the closure as a milestone amid ongoing political tensions and international funding cuts.
