Good morning. It is Friday, October nine. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. In the New Patriotic Party, newly elected national chairman John Boadu has led party executives to visit former President John Kufuor to seek counsel and build party unity ahead of the twenty twenty-eight elections, according to Three News. The Ghana Report adds that Member of Parliament Charles Forson has dismissed concerns that past electoral rivalry between John Boadu and general secretary Justin Kodua Frimpong will hurt their work, saying both leaders have the experience and structures to work together effectively.

Story two. President John Mahama has emphasized that the government will continue delivering reliable power and sound economic conditions to drive industrial transformation, Three News reports. The president made the remarks in Tema during the commissioning of Nestlé Ghana's expanded evaporated milk production line, which increases factory capacity to thirty thousand tonnes annually.

Story three. Foreign Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa has announced that Chinese technical experts are due to arrive in Ghana to review major infrastructure projects, reports Three News. The projects under review include the Damongo University, the Aflao Market, and the National Theatre.

Story four. The National Democratic Congress has closed the nomination period for its Greater Accra regional elections, with about eight hundred and thirty aspirants picking forms to contest various positions, according to The Ghana Report. Aspirants must now complete the filing process before the regional polls take place.

Now, around the world.

Story five. Violent anti-migrant protests have erupted in South Africa, with cars torched and shops looted in Soweto and Durban, according to the B B C and Africanews. The unrest broke out following a Constitutional Court ruling that eased procedural restrictions on asylum applications, intensifying longstanding tensions against foreign nationals.

Story six. Ethiopian government forces have carried out a drone attack against Eritrean troops who crossed into Ethiopia's northern Tigray region, The Guardian reports. The military vehicles had crossed the southern border and reached the town of Adigrat, raising fears of a wider regional conflict between the two neighbouring countries.
