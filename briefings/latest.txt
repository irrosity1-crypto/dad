Good evening. It is Wednesday, October twenty-eighth, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. The New Patriotic Party has filed a suit at the Supreme Court seeking an interpretation of the constitutional rules on presidential term limits, as reported by both Graphic Online and Joy Online. Graphic Online reports that the suit aims to determine whether the two-term limit means consecutive or non-consecutive terms, bringing clarity to debates over whether former President John Dramani Mahama could run again in twenty twenty-eight. Meanwhile, Joy Online reports that former President John Agyekum Kufuor has separately voiced support for extending Ghana's presidential term from four years to five years to give governments adequate time for their policies.

Story two. Graphic Online reports that the Minority in Parliament is demanding the immediate resignation of the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa, over the evacuation of nearly two thousand Ghanaians from South Africa. The Minority Ranking Member on the Foreign Affairs Committee, Samuel Abdulai Jinapor, cited alleged financial irregularities amounting to over forty-nine million Ghana cedis, calling for an independent audit and threatening a vote of censure if the minister does not step down.

Story three. Former Tamale Central Member of Parliament Inusah Fuseini has warned against the rising use of money to buy votes and manipulate internal party elections, reporting Joy Online. He stressed that financial inducements are threatening electoral integrity and driving honest people out of public life. In response to the growing problem, Joy Online also reports that the National Democratic Congress is currently considering new internal regulations to separate legitimate transport payments for delegates from illegal political inducements.

Now, around the world.

Story four. Three News and Al Jazeera report that Russian President Vladimir Putin has addressed the third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow, telling African leaders that Russia is the continent's strongest partner. Speaking directly to visiting delegations, President Putin contrasted his country's partnership with what he described as the colonial approach of the West and pledged support to African nations facing global challenges.

Story five. The head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has sounded an international alarm over an unprecedented Ebola outbreak that has surpassed ten thousand cases and claimed nearly five thousand lives, according to The Guardian and Africanews. Doctor Jean Kaseya warned that the virus, which is heavily concentrated in the Democratic Republic of Congo, risks spreading globally like the coronavirus unless international funding for treatment and preparedness is rapidly provided.
