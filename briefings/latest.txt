Good morning. Today is Wednesday, September 30, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. A major political dispute has erupted in Parliament after the Speaker, Alban Bagbin, dismissed a Private Member’s motion from the Minority seeking a parliamentary inquiry into recent high-profile narcotics seizures linked to Ghana. Graphic Online reports that Speaker Bagbin ruled the motion inadmissible because its proposed inquiry overlaps with ongoing court cases, creating a risk of prejudice. However, MyJoyOnline reports that Deputy Minority Chief Whip Habib Iddrisu strongly challenged this decision, expressing surprise and arguing that ongoing court cases should not prevent Parliament from exercising its constitutional oversight. Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin also criticized the ruling, accusing the Speaker and the Majority of weakening Parliament's oversight powers. Conversely, 3News reports that Majority Leader James Agalga defended the dismissal, calling the Minority's motion premature and stating that Parliament does not conduct criminal investigations.

Story two. Ghana has won a major international tax dispute against oil producer Tullow Oil. According to 3News, an international arbitration tribunal dismissed all claims brought by the company and upheld a three hundred and ninety-three point zero nine million U.S. dollar tax assessment issued by the Ghana Revenue Authority.

Story three. In party politics, the National Democratic Congress has announced that it will open nominations for its twenty twenty-six Regional Executive Elections on Tuesday, October 6. The Ghana Report says this official announcement begins the process of selecting new regional leaders. Meanwhile, a new national outlook survey by Kumasi Technical University and Feedback Africa reveals that former General Secretary John Boadu is currently leading the race for the New Patriotic Party's upcoming National Chairmanship election. 

Now, around the world.

Story four. In Morocco, King Mohammed the Sixth has appointed Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri as the country's first-ever female prime minister following recent elections. BBC News Africa reports that El Mansouri, who previously served as the mayor of Marrakesh and as a cabinet minister, is only the second woman to lead a government in the Arab world. She has pledged to tackle pressing national issues, including high unemployment and rising living costs.

Story five. United States President Donald Trump hosted a "Super Intelligence" summit at the White House with leading artificial intelligence executives. France twenty-four reports that President Trump announced the tech leaders signed a "morally binding" commitment to self-regulate and build safeguards, stating that the companies will police themselves. This comes amid mounting global concerns over the safety and rapid advancement of artificial intelligence models.
