Good evening. It is Tuesday night, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. In a developing scandal, the interdicted headmaster of Obuasi Senior High Technical School, Zakaria Abdulai, has resigned as the Juaben constituency secretary for the National Democratic Congress and withdrawn from the constituency chairmanship race. The party's national leadership, through General Secretary Fifi Fiavi Kwetey, had earlier directed him to step aside to allow unhindered police inquiries into allegations of sexual misconduct involving female students. Three News now reports that Interior Minister Muntaka Mubarak has confirmed the headmaster's arrest, while Mr Abdulai maintains his innocence and says he will pursue all lawful avenues to clear his name.

Story two. The flagbearer of the New Patriotic Party, Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia, has called on the government to translate reported economic gains into real relief for ordinary Ghanaian households. Speaking at the party's National Thanksgiving Service, he accused the administration of failing to deliver on key campaign promises and argued that proper economic management must be felt in the cost of rent, fuel, transport, and utilities, Graphic Online reports.

Story three. The Coalition of Domestic Election Observers has urged the government to reform the appointment process for the leadership of the Electoral Commission. According to Graphic Online, the group expressed concern that the government's Position Paper rejected recommendations from the Constitution Review Committee, which had called for an open, competitive process involving a reformed Council of State, the Public Services Commission, and parliamentary approval.

Story four. Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin has initiated fresh legal action, filing defamation suits at the Accra High Court against Sammy Gyamfi, the Chief Executive of the Ghana Gold Board, and Eric Adjei, the head of the National Entrepreneurship and Innovation Programme. This comes shortly after Starr F M reported that the Minority Leader had initially withdrawn a previous two-million-cedi suit against Mr Adjei following a consensus between the two parties.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In Sudan, local officials and medical sources report that military drone strikes have killed scores of people in the disputed border region of Abyei. According to the B B C and France Twenty-Four, the strikes hit a market, causing a massive blaze at a fuel depot, and also targeted a truck carrying mourners to a funeral, with casualties reported to be over one hundred and fifty people.

Story six. In Nigeria, the government has confirmed its first case of Ebola linked to the recent outbreak on the continent. Deutsche Welle and Africanews report that the patient is a woman who recently flew into the federal capital of Abuja from Uganda, and she has since been placed in isolation while receiving medical treatment.
