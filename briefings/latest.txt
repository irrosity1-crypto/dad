Good evening. It is Monday night, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. The opposition New Patriotic Party is demanding accountability from the government over the evacuation of Ghanaians escaping xenophobic violence in South Africa. As reported by Graphic Online and Starr F M, the party says documents show the Foreign Affairs Ministry paid seven hundred and fifty thousand dollars per charter flight, spending a total of twenty-seven million Ghana cedis across three flights without providing invoices, an amount the N P P insists is far above industry rates.

Story two. The Coalition of Domestic Election Observers, known as C O D E O, has urged newly appointed Electoral Commission Deputy Chairperson Emelia Affi Agbenyo to resign from her post. Graphic Online reports that while the group acknowledges the President's appointing powers, it argues her active campaigning for the National Democratic Congress during the twenty twenty-four election compromises public trust, alongside calls from the Catholic Bishops' Conference for new commissioners to remain strictly non-partisan.

Story three. Tension is brewing within the National Democratic Congress as internal party contest preparations face challenges in the regions. According to MyJoyOnline, the Tertiary Education Institutions Network has appealed to national chairman Johnson Asiedu Nketia after former campus executives were disqualified from regional races, while the party's women's wing in Atwima Kwanwoma has raised alarms over an allegedly bloated voters' register ahead of constituency polls.

Story four. President John Dramani Mahama has outlined a new Ghana Gold Board policy intended to expand local testing and refining, aiming to retain more mineral revenue inside the country, according to Three News. Meanwhile, the National Association of Concerned Clergy has approached both the President and Parliament, asking government leadership to clarify the official status of the country's anti-L G B T Q plus legislation.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In Kenya, a court has convicted starvation cult leader Paul Mackenzie and about thirty followers for the murder of one hundred and ninety-one people, nearly all of them children, as reported widely by the B B C, Al Jazeera, and The Guardian. The judgment follows the discovery of mass graves in the Shakahola forest after authorities uncovered that the preacher had instructed church members to starve themselves and their families to death.

Story six. A devastating drone strike in southern Sudan has killed eighty people near a transport hub and market in the town of al-Dibab, according to The Guardian. The strike hits during ongoing warfare across the nation, with witnesses reporting that two consecutive strikes hit commuters as they gathered for transport home in West Kordofan state.
