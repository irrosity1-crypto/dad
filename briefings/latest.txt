Good morning, and happy Thursday. 

First, the news from home. 

Story one. MyJoyOnline reports that former Finance Minister Doctor Mohammed Amin Adam has criticized the government's voluntary Domestic Debt Exchange Programme bond buyback. He argued that bondholders have already sacrificed enough under the initial debt exchange and warned that the early buyback could disadvantage them. 

Story two. The Ghana Report and MyJoyOnline note that the National Youth Organiser of the New Patriotic Party, Abanga Yakubu Fusani, has issued a strong warning against any unconstitutional third term for President John Dramani Mahama. He described the push by some ruling party members as a recipe for chaos and pure greed. 

Story three. Graphic Online and Starr FM report that fifteen out of the eighteen active chapter chairpersons of the New Patriotic Party in the U S A have called for the suspension of their branch elections scheduled for November 28. They want the Elections Committee to answer questions in writing regarding electronic voting safeguards before proceeding. 

Now, around the world. 

Story four. Africanews reports that a South African court has ruled in favor of President Cyril Ramaphosa, throwing out an impeachment report linked to cash theft at his private farm. The court victory removes a major political threat hanging over his presidency. 

Story five. B B C News Africa reports that the Democratic Republic of Congo has demanded answers from Uganda after a general made controversial social media threats about taking over a mineral-rich area in the region. The comments have heightened diplomatic tensions between the two neighbors.
