First, the news from home. There are only two political stories from Ghana today. 

Story one. The New Patriotic Party, the N P P, has opened applications for prospective parliamentary candidates seeking to contest its parliamentary primaries ahead of the 2028 general elections. According to MyJoyOnline, the party has set an application fee of five thousand Ghana cedis for interested aspirants.

Story two. 3News reports that the District Chief Executive for Atwima Mponua and the Deputy Chief Executive Officer of the Ghana Integrated Aluminium Development Corporation have held discussions with investors regarding bauxite deposits in Nyinahin. The district capital's bauxite reserves are estimated at seventeen point five billion dollars, with the potential to create nearly ninety-eight thousand jobs.

Story three. France 24 reports that the United Nations peacekeeping mission in the disputed Western Sahara region has ended after thirty-five years. The mission closed after the Security Council failed to renew its mandate, leading Morocco to welcome the decision while the Algeria-backed Polisario Front strongly condemned it.

Story four. According to The Guardian, British officials insisted that discussions on slavery reparations be kept off the formal agenda of the Commonwealth leaders' summit. Caribbean political figure Ralph Gonsalves revealed that the United Kingdom pushed back against including the topic, though the British prime minister has since agreed to informal discussions on reparatory justice.
