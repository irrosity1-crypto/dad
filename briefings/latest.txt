Good evening, and welcome to your political news briefing for this Sunday. 

First, the news from home. 

Story one. According to MyJoyOnline and Graphic Online, the National Democratic Congress has been holding its constituency executive elections across the country over the weekend. While areas like Juaboso saw all twenty-four positions go unopposed, other places like Techiman South saw several incumbent executives lose their positions. Greater Accra Regional Chairman Emmanuel Nii Ashie Moore has urged party members to put their differences aside and unite behind the party following the polls.

Story two. Graphic Online and Three News report that former President John Dramani Mahama presented the state of peace and security in Africa report at the twelfth Tana High-Level Forum in Ethiopia. He called for a new regional security accord to strengthen cooperation among West African countries and urged the African Union to hold governments accountable for failing to implement peace measures. He also emphasized that lasting peace on the continent requires economic sovereignty and self-reliance.

Story three. The Ghana Report notes that Dr Ekua Amoakoh, spokesperson for New Patriotic Party flagbearer Dr Mahamudu Bawumia, has accused the N D C of changing its position on the formalisation of small-scale mining. She stated in an interview that the N P P had previously called for unlicensed small-scale miners to be brought into the formal sector after returning to power. 

Now, around the world.

Story four. The Guardian and France 24 report that voters in Brazil are casting their ballots today in a highly unpredictable presidential runoff election. Incumbent left-wing President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva is attempting an unprecedented comeback against right-wing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, who secured a surprise victory in the first round. The campaign has been heavily dominated by corruption scandals and economic difficulties.

Story five. Three News and France 24 report that former United States President Barack Obama has hit the campaign trail to rally support for Democrats ahead of the upcoming midterm elections. Speaking in Ohio, Obama strongly criticized Donald Trump over his handling of the economy and the war with Iran, urging voters to hold the administration accountable.
