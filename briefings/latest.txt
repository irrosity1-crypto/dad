Good evening. It is five o'clock on Tuesday, and here is your political news update.

First, the news from home.

Story one. The headmaster of Obuasi Senior High Technical School, Zakaria Abdulai, has resigned as the N D C Juaben Constituency Secretary and withdrawn from the constituency chairmanship race amid police investigations into alleged sexual abuse. According to reports from Graphic Online, Starr F M, and MyJoyOnline, the N D C national leadership had earlier directed him to step aside to allow unhindered police inquiries, with party leaders warning that severe sanctions will follow if the allegations are proven true.

Story two. The flag bearer of the N P P, Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia, has called on the government to ensure its reported economic achievements result in actual relief for ordinary citizens. Speaking at an N P P thanksgiving service as reported by Graphic Online, Doctor Bawumia argued that true economic success must be judged by the affordability of essentials like fuel, rent, and utilities rather than official statistics alone.

Story three. The Coalition of Domestic Election Observers has urged the government to reform how the leadership of the Electoral Commission is appointed. Graphic Online reports that the observer group is pushing for an open, competitive process that includes parliamentary approval, expressing disappointment that the government has rejected proposed changes to the existing system.

Story four. President John Mahama has announced that two hundred million Ghana cedis will be allocated in the twenty twenty-seven budget to support illegal mining operations and protect the country's water bodies. According to Starr F M, the dedicated funds will go toward strengthening the National Alternative Employment and Livelihood Programme and the Blue Water Guards.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In Sudan, deadly military drone strikes have hit a funeral gathering and a fuel market, reportedly killing hundreds of people. The B B C reports that local sources claim over three hundred people died in the attacks, while France Twenty Four notes that at least one hundred and fifty-three were killed in the disputed Abyei border region.

Story six. Regional tensions are escalating in the Horn of Africa as Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed warned neighboring Eritrea of severe consequences. According to Africanews, Prime Minister Abiy cautioned the Eritrean government to immediately cease its alleged support for rebel groups operating inside Ethiopia.
