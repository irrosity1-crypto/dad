Good evening, today is Wednesday, October twenty-first. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. Vice-President Professor Jane Naana Opoku-Agyemang has addressed the sexual assault case involving the headmaster of Obuasi Senior High Technical School, describing it as a serious national matter and demanding that the law take its course. Graphic Online and Starr F M report that her remarks come amid rising public concern, while the N P P in Amasaman has called on the police to investigate remarks made on the matter by an N D C communications officer. 

Story two. Deputy General Secretary Mustapha Gbande and National Organiser Joseph Yammin have filed their nominations to retain their posts on the National Executive Board of the governing N D C ahead of the December elections. MyJoyOnline also reports that governance spokesperson at the presidency, Doctor Sammy Ayeh, has picked up nomination forms to contest for the post of Deputy National Communications Officer.

Story three. Ghana's Court of Appeal has dismissed a bid by Kwame Baffoe, popularly known as Abronye, to halt a defamation lawsuit brought against him by Samson Lardy Anyenini. According to Starr F M, the court rejected the application and awarded costs of ten thousand Ghana cedis against Abronye.

Story four. Opposition Member of Parliament Kojo Oppong Nkrumah has challenged the government to account for an estimated six hundred and fifty billion Ghana cedis in public funds received during its first two years in office. Graphic Online reports that the lawmaker insists the administration must show clear results before rolling out any new economic programme.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In Sudan, local authorities say the death toll from an army drone strike on a market in the disputed Abyei region has climbed to at least one hundred and fifty-three people. France Twenty-Four reports that the attack on the Amiet market involved multiple munitions, drawing severe criticism from conflict monitors over the lack of regard for civilian lives in the ongoing war.

Story six. Russia has signalled it is ready in principle to resume trilateral talks with Ukraine and the United States to end the war, following a visit to Saint Petersburg by Donald Trump's special envoy. The B B C and France Twenty-Four report that while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has backed holding discussions soon, Moscow also released jailed American Stephen Hubbard during the diplomatic exchange.
