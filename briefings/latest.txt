Story one: Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin has challenged the governing N D C to accept the same accountability standards it demanded while in opposition, insisting parliamentary scrutiny of public spending will continue. In response, People's National Convention General Secretary Awudu Ishaq accused the Minority of pursuing partisan interests, while N D C communicator Nichiren Appiah Buadi supported an audit into allegations against Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa, according to reports from MyJoyOnline and Starr F M.

Story two: Three News reports that the Economic and Organised Crime Office, or E O C O, is seeking the court-ordered confiscation of assets belonging to Chairman Wontumi. The assets targeted by the investigative agency include fifty million Ghana cedis in Treasury Bills and his Akonta Mining concession.

Story three: Three News also reports that President John Dramani Mahama has held bilateral talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin at the Russia-Africa Summit to push for stronger economic ties. During his visit, President Mahama also told Ghanaians in Russia that the country currently has adequate power supply and has put an end to power outages.

Story four: The Judicial Service Staff Association of Ghana, known as J U S A G, has declared an indefinite strike starting November 2 over the non-review of salaries. Three News reports that the union announced the action following unsuccessful attempts since December 2025 to secure the pay review guaranteed under the constitution.
