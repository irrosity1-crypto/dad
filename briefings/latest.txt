Good morning. It is Monday today. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. Both MyJoyOnline and Starr F M report that the Minority caucus in Parliament is demanding an independent audit of government spending on the evacuation exercise from South Africa. The caucus says records from the Foreign Affairs Ministry show more than sixteen point four million Ghana cedis requiring further explanation out of the total forty-nine point seven two million cedis spent. 

Story two. According to reports from Starr F M and The Ghana Report, the New Patriotic Party has warned prospective parliamentary aspirants, their agents, and supporters to stop campaigning immediately. A statement signed by General Secretary Justin Kodua Frimpong warned that anyone who violates the directive could face disciplinary sanctions and lose the opportunity to contest in the party primaries. 

Story three. Three News reports that the Minister for Foreign Affairs, Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa, has dismissed fears that Ghana's application to join B R I C S will hurt its relations with traditional partner countries. Speaking on diplomatic developments, the minister also announced that Cabinet has approved a long-pending diplomatic agreement between Ghana and the Holy See, and noted positive progress in security partnerships with the United Kingdom.

Story four. Three News also reports that Finance Minister Dr. Cassiel Ato Forson is leading Ghana's delegation to Bangkok for the twenty twenty-six International Monetary Fund and World Bank Annual Meetings. The minister is attending the global finance gathering ahead of the rollout of the government's New Economy blueprint.

Now, around the world.

Story five. The B B C and France Twenty-Four report that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has offered conditional support for an energy infrastructure ceasefire announced by United States President Donald Trump, provided Russia honours it. However, Ukrainian officials and European allies noted that Russian forces struck an energy plant immediately after the truce was declared, leaving doubts over whether the deal will hold.

Story six. B B C News Africa and Deutsche Welle report that air strikes on an artisanal gold mining camp along the disputed Egypt-Sudan border have killed dozens of people, with a Sudanese rights group putting the death toll at seventy-two. Neither the Egyptian nor the Sudanese government has officially commented on the attacks on the remote border area.
