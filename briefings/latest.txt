Good morning. Today is Thursday, October 1.

First, the news from home.

Story one. The High Court in Accra has granted the Economic and Organised Crime Office, known as E O C O, a warrant to arrest the Member of Parliament for Manhyia South, Nana Agyei Baffour Awuah. Starr F M and 3 News report that the lawmaker is wanted over allegations of money laundering, financial loss, and the dissipation of public funds. MyJoyOnline reports that government spokesperson Felix Kwakye Ofosu has rejected claims that officers tried to kidnap the lawmaker, stating that being an M P should not shield anyone from law enforcement.

Story two. The New Patriotic Party, or N P P, has announced the voting schedule and identification requirements for its national officers' elections this Saturday. According to MyJoyOnline and The Ghana Report, recent polls show former General Secretary John Boadu leading the race for National Chairman, while incumbent Justin Kodua Frimpong leads the General Secretary contest. Meanwhile, Vice-Chairperson hopeful Henry Nana Boakye has defended his candidacy, arguing that his young age should not be mistaken for a lack of experience.

Story three. Speaker of Parliament Alban Bagbin and Chief of Staff Dr Debrah have addressed the maiden conference of the Ghana Law Society. 3 News reports that Speaker Bagbin warned against institutional rivalry between the new society and the Ghana Bar Association, saying the country does not need a divided legal profession. Dr Debrah added that lawyers' groups must not become extensions of partisan contests and called for faster justice in commercial cases to support a twenty-four-hour economy.

Now, around the world.

Story four. Burundi has agreed to receive third-country migrants deported from the United States under Donald Trump's hardline immigration policies. The Guardian reports that Burundi joins several other African nations in accepting these deportees, though a presidential spokesperson clarified they will only accept individuals who are not accused of crimes or terrorism.

Story five. In South Africa, President Cyril Ramaphosa has called on men to take responsibility for gender-based violence following a series of murders of women. According to The Guardian and Africanews, the president announced that police will review unsolved cases, while hundreds of anti-migrant protesters in Durban have held demonstrations demanding that undocumented immigrants leave the country.
