Good evening. It is Sunday, October eighteenth, and here is your political news briefing.

First, the news from home.

Story one. In our top story, reported widely by Graphic Online and MyJoyOnline, the New Patriotic Party and the government are clashing over the repatriation of Ghanaians from South Africa. The Communications Director of the N P P, Dennis Miracles Aboagye, has raised questions about payment records, alleging discrepancies in the figures that cast doubt on whether all beneficiaries received their full allowances. In response, Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa has insisted that not a single pesewa is missing, rejecting Minority claims that more than sixteen million cedis could not be accounted for.

Story two. The presidential candidate of the N P P, Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia, has criticised the current state of Ghana's economy under the N D C government, according to MyJoyOnline. Speaking on the nation's financial health, he argued that Ghana is experiencing two distinct economies, a paper economy and a pocket economy. Doctor Bawumia insisted that economic stability is completely meaningless unless it directly improves the living standards and finances of ordinary citizens.

Story three. In other political developments reported by Graphic Online and MyJoyOnline, the N P P has held a national thanksgiving service at the Seventh-day Adventist Church in Goaso to celebrate its recent internal elections and the selection of its flagbearer. The event comes as the Young Executive Forum issued a statement congratulating Alfred Kojo Thompson on his election as the party's new National Treasurer. 

Story four. Parliament's Foreign Affairs Committee has also been in the news, with MyJoyOnline reporting that Ranking Member Samuel Abu Jinapor has questioned the government's pursuit of a partnership with the B R I C S group of nations. The lawmaker described the government's expected economic gains from joining the bloc as entirely unrealistic.

Now, around the world.

Story five. In our first world story, reported by several outlets including France twenty-four, the B B C, and The Guardian, France has been gripped by widespread turmoil following nationwide protests over the rising cost of living that turned into intense riots. French President Emmanuel Macron addressed the nation on Sunday, condemning the unbridled violence and looting that have resulted in more than two thousand arrests. 

Story six. On our continent, Africanews reports that Cameroon's main opposition party, the Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon, has chosen a new leader. Tiriane Balbine Noah was elected party president to replace Maurice Kamto, making her the first woman to head the prominent opposition movement.
