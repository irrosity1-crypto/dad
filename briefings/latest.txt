Good morning. It is Monday today. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. Across MyJoyOnline, Graphic Online, and Starr F M, the major talking point remains the aftermath of the New Patriotic Party national executive elections, where John Boadu has been elected national chairman. Former minister Boakye Agyarko has congratulated Mr Boadu and pledged full support to unite the party ahead of the twenty twenty-eight polls, while the opposition National Democratic Congress has also sent formal congratulations, urging that the national interest be put above party politics. Meanwhile, delegates produced a major upset by electing an auto mechanic and former grassroots activist, Kwadwo Fosu Boadu, popular as Kojo Bamba, as first vice chairman over several high-profile contenders.

Story two. Graphic Online reports that the New Patriotic Party has amended its constitution to formally name its presidential candidate as the official leader of the party, or the sitting head of state whenever the party is in government. Following this reform, flagbearer Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia announced that the party has already begun drawing up its twenty twenty-nine governance programme nearly three years early, as reported by Graphic Online and MyJoyOnline, and has also started scouting potential parliamentary candidates who have the strength to win seats rather than relying only on internal primaries.

Story three. Three News reports that the Minister for Education, Haruna Iddrisu, has visited a data processing centre where the government is working to validate the records of fifty-one thousand teachers caught up in the ongoing pre-tertiary strike. The minister urged the validation team to speed up their work so that the government can meet its timeline and release the first batch of salary arrears to the teachers by October sixteenth.

Story four. The Ghana Report reveals that former Speaker of Parliament Professor Aaron Mike Oquaye has disclosed how he previously intervened to stop the Office of the Special Prosecutor from arresting the Member of Parliament for Bawku Central, Mahama Ayariga. Professor Oquaye explained that his actions during his tenure as Speaker were not motivated by party politics, but were taken to defend the constitutional rights of the lawmaker and protect the authority of Parliament.

Now, around the world.

Story five. The B B C, The Guardian, and France Twenty-Four report that Brazil is heading for a presidential election run-off on October twenty-fifth after right-wing candidate Flavio Bolsonaro took a surprise first-round lead over incumbent president Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva. Neither candidate secured the fifty percent required for an outright victory, setting up a fierce second-round contest between the forty-five-year-old son of former president Jair Bolsonaro and the sitting left-wing leader.

Story six. In regional African affairs, B B C News Africa and Africanews report that Ethiopian federal forces have taken control of Mekelle, the regional capital of Tigray, following the withdrawal of rebel fighters from the city. The development marks a major shift in the northern conflict, as regional neighbours met in Egypt to discuss the broader risks to regional stability.
