Good morning, it is Thursday, October eight. 

First, the news from home.

Story one. The New Patriotic Party is mourning the passing of its former National Chairman, Ambassador Samuel Arthur Odoi-Sykes, who died at the age of eighty-seven. Graphic Online, MyJoyOnline, and Three News report that tributes have poured in from party leaders, including Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia, who praised him as a founding pillar who led the N P P to its first election victory in the Fourth Republic in the year two thousand.

Story two. Ghana is moving forward with plans to join the B R I C S geopolitical bloc. According to Three News and Africanews, Cabinet has given the green light for a formal application, with lawmaker Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa stating that the move reflects the government's determination to position Ghana as a reliable friend of all nations.

Story three. In education and party politics, MyJoyOnline reports that Education Minister Haruna Iddrisu has strongly rejected claims by N P P flagbearer Doctor Mahamudu Bawumia that the Free Senior High School policy has suffered major setbacks. Speaking in response to Doctor Bawumia's remarks at the N P P National Delegates Conference, the minister defended the governing N D C's management of the flagship programme.

Story four. Parliament faces fresh political friction as Starr F M reports that the Minority caucus is demanding the removal of Sports Minister Kofi Adams and Ghana Tourism Authority Chief Executive Maame Afua Houadjeto. The opposition demands their exit over alleged irregularities surrounding Ghana's twenty twenty-six World Cup visa arrangements.

Now, around the world.

Story five. Turning to the Horn of Africa, the B B C reports that residents in the northern Ethiopian town of Adigrat have witnessed truckloads of Eritrean soldiers moving deep into the Tigray region. This reported military movement raises fresh security concerns in an area still recovering from devastating conflict.

Story six. In South Sudan, preparations for the upcoming December twenty-two general election face severe hurdles. D W and Africanews report that growing insecurity, political tensions, and delayed voter registration have sparked widespread doubts about the poll, prompting high-level talks between South Sudanese President Salva Kiir and South African President Cyril Ramaphosa to salvage the peace process.
